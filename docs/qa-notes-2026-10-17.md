## What the speaker said (interpretation)
1. **Multi-method, not Raman only.**
   - Spectra of real process samples are superimposed (overlapping signals of many components).
   - Combining several spectroscopy methods (and spectral libraries) gives more accurate data than one method.
   - Commercial systems exist but are not complete.
   - "You don't need it for a cartoon right now" (?) probably means "not needed for the hackathon right now, but keep it in mind".
2. **Real-time process control, not end-point checks.**
   - For any new reaction or new drug, you must follow what changes during the process, including intermediates. If you only check the final product, much can happen in between that changes the composition.
   - Analogy: drive the car yourself all the way, not only check at departure and arrival; anything can happen on the road.
   - Real-time spectroscopy with several methods shows more precisely which chemical components change.
3. **Chemometrics is the key term.**
   - Process diagnostics uses several methods, then chemometric software: mathematical treatment of spectra to get chemical composition, not just intensity changes at some wavelengths ("this violence" probably means "these wavelengths" (?)).
   - Several companies sell chemometric software and models, but you always need data (calibration and reference measurements) before modelling, and then the question is how correct the answer is. That is part of AFS's work.
   - We should understand what (multispectral) chemometrics means. "Reliable not always sustainable" (?): unclear.
4. **Evaluation.** The jury evaluates H01–H06 (the specified cases) and may also ask us to run a short new enquiry live, as stated in the brief. This matches the earlier "both" decision.
5. **Practical applications.** About 10 cases, for example:
   - soil;
   - paracetamol tablet dissolution in water: tracking paracetamol and another component over time (e.g. within 10–30 min), which matters for drug dissolution testing.
6. **Business direction.**
   - Move from large multispectral diagnostic lab systems to small, cheap, dedicated sensors ("multivalence census" (?) probably means multi-wavelength / multivariate sensors).
   - Chemometric model software makes this possible; IoT and cloud data let a process be watched remotely, e.g. on a phone.
   - Prices were mentioned; **never put them in outputs** (not from the supplied materials; challenge rule).
7. **Customers.** Not mass production of sensors. AFS works on the customer's problem as research: smart diagnostics of a particular process to find where the problem or bottleneck is, like a diagnosis before treatment. They look for customers with trouble.
8. **Expected depth of the first feasibility proposal.** A proposal and a comparison design between methods is the right level. A concrete plan with sample numbers and acceptance criteria "would be too much to expect".
9. **Methods.**
   - Absorption spectroscopy is the basis of most analyses (the speaker's own share figure; do not cite it). Raman is "booming", partly fashion.
   - Raman reflects symmetric molecular vibrations and IR absorption asymmetric ones, so the two are complementary.
   - Compare methods. If one method answers the question well enough, two or four are not needed; only if it is not enough, combine.
