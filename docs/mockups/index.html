Mockups for tasks 5.10 (layout and hierarchy) and 5.11 (academic fonts, setup diagram drawn as a blueprint). They are static and nothing in the app has changed. Every direction shows the same slice of the answer page, in order: question, recommendation, setup drawing, options, and one expanded detail section.

Content source: all text, IDs and citations are copied from the saved dev output outputs/dev/D03.json (option O2, recommended). The drawing was written by hand for this mockup. In the app it would still be drawn by code from the option.

The drawing's callouts link to the sections. Each callout on the drawing (P1–P3, M1–M2, Q3) carries the same ID as the parameter, method or open question it points to below. A shows the hover state, where P2 lights up on the drawing and in the table together.

Your question
Our dairy process includes a relatively clear whey stream before a concentration stage. We want to investigate whether spectroscopy can track protein concentration as the stream changes, with laboratory results used as the reference. The stream can become more turbid under some operating conditions. We need a first measurement concept that handles this change rather than assuming the liquid always remains clear.
1Our recommendation

Start with NIR transmission/transflection.

The sources name whey as a preferred case for it, and it is the least effort. Because the stream can turn turbid, keep mid-IR ATR as the fallback and treat a fused NIR plus mid-IR ATR model as a later hypothesis. The protein range, required accuracy and the nature of the turbidity are still open.

Confidence
Medium · open question: Q1, Q2

What would change it

What we still need to know

  2. Q2How turbid does the stream become, and what causes the turbidity (particles, fat or dissolved species)?Why it matters: The severity and nature decide whether NIR transmission still works or whether ATR is needed.

Reads like a paper. The citations move into a margin column with their verified quotes, Tufte-style, the parameter table is a booktabs table, and the drawing is a numbered figure with a caption. The cyanotype plate (white lines on Prussian blue) is the most recognisable “blueprint”. The cost is that serif body text at small sizes on low-DPI screens is softer, and the layout is the furthest from today’s.

Adobe’s Source superfamily was designed as one system, and its sans has tabular lining figures by default. A numbered rail (01–05) runs from the recommendation through the drawing to the details. Under the drawing, a key strip sends each callout group (M, P, Q) to its section, so the drawing visibly “flows into” the page. This direction has the strongest hierarchy and the most modern feel.
