# Short brief: Biomass related monitoring during yeast fermentation

*Short view built by code from the saved run JSON (no model call). Full answer: [D05.md](../D05.md).*

**Decision status:** Provisional feasibility assessment

Test first a Raman probe to resolve specific metabolites in the aqueous broth, alongside a UV-Vis ATR probe for the biomass growth trend as optical density rises. Single-method trials will establish whether Raman alone or UV-Vis alone suffices before testing a combined setup. Key open points are the defined metabolic targets, bioreactor installation constraints, and your numerical go/no-go criteria.

## Requirements

**Stated by the customer:** "yeast fermentation"; "The broth becomes more optically dense as the run develops."; "a biomass signal"; "a metabolic signal"

**Other key unknowns:** Installation point, probe interface and sterilisation requirements in the bioreactor; Required measurement frequency and response time.

## Clarification questions

1. Which metabolites should the 'metabolic signal' track (e.g. glucose, acetate, lactate, ethanol), and over what concentration ranges?
2. Is the metabolic change expected to involve fluorescent species such as NADH, tryptophan or riboflavin, and can an off-line fluorescence scan of the broth be run?
3. How quickly must changes be detected, and at what measurement frequency?

## Measurement concept

Immersion UV-Vis ATR probe for the biomass/turbidity trend, and a Raman immersion probe for the metabolic fingerprint; both placed at the same sampling point or as close as the reactor ports allow. **Location:** In-line in the bioreactor broth, at a position representative of the bulk (avoiding dead zones), to be fixed with the customer. **Fiber link:** Silica fibers for UV-Vis and Raman; the usable spectral range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe actually selected, which is not yet defined. [S01 p.16; S01 p.15]

## Spectral range

- **Raman:** Raman is reported as Raman shift in cm-1 with the laser excitation wavelength stated separately; the excitation wavelength for this application is not yet selected. [S01 p.11]

## Risks and uncertainty

- The undefined 'metabolic signal' may turn out to be a trace or non-Raman-active species, invalidating Raman as the primary metabolic technique. (related AFS source: S01 p.16, "may lack the sensitivity to detect trace compounds below 0.1 percent."; not checked against this sentence)
- Probe windows (ATR crystal, Raman optics) may foul in the fermenter, degrading the signal over long runs. (related AFS source: S01 p.13, "typically at a depth of few microns"; not checked against this sentence)
- Fluorescence background from broth components can swamp the weak Raman signal and must be checked on real samples. [S01 p.11]

## Feasibility plan

- **Next step:** Define the target metabolites and go/no-go criteria with the customer, then perform single-method laboratory trials on representative broth samples with independent reference measurements.
- **Reference measurements:** Off-line cell density / cell dry weight, HPLC or enzymatic metabolite assays, and an off-line fluorescence scan of the broth.
- **Decision basis:** Customer-defined numerical accuracy criteria per target, assessed on held-out runs or batches; no criteria are stated in the enquiry, so these must be agreed.
