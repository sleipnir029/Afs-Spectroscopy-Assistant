# Short brief: API concentration during hot polymer extrusion

*Short view built by code from the saved run JSON (no model call). Full answer: [D07.md](../D07.md).*

**Decision status:** Provisional feasibility assessment

Test MIR-ATR first: it is the method with a direct in-line polymer extrusion precedent, with Raman as the comparison method on the same melt samples. NIR reflection is conditional on the melt being opaque; NIR transmission and fluorescence are not recommended for now. Open points: polymer/API identity and concentration range, melt temperature and pressure, melt opacity, required response time, and probe access and fouling behaviour at the hot interface.

## Requirements

**Stated by the customer:** "We are developing a hot polymer extrusion process that incorporates an active pharmaceutical ingredient."; "We have offline assays"; "the process interface is hot and may be exposed to material build up."; "detect composition changes while the melt is being processed."

**Other key unknowns:** Melt temperature and pressure at the intended measurement point; Available access point geometry (flange, sapphire window, bypass) and acceptable probe contact.

## Clarification questions

1. Which polymer and which API are used, and over what concentration range must composition changes be detected?
2. Is the melt optically transparent or opaque/scattering at process conditions?
3. How fast must composition changes be detected (response time and measurement frequency)?

## Measurement concept

Immersion ATR fiber probe (diamond or zirconium dioxide tip) in tight contact with the melt at a die or barrel port; Raman probe as a comparison, optionally through a window; NIR reflection probe only if the melt is opaque. **Location:** Extruder die or barrel access point downstream of the melting/mixing zone, close to where composition is representative. **Fiber link:** MIR-ATR requires CIR or PIR fibers because silica fibers do not cover the mid-IR range; Raman and NIR use silica fibers. Achievable MIR link length is an open question; long cables are only documented for silica. [S02 p.6; S02 p.7]

## Spectral range

- **MIR:** Nominal mid-IR range is 4000-400 cm-1; the usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe actually selected. [S01 p.9]
- **Raman:** Raman shift in cm-1 with the laser excitation wavelength (e.g. 785 nm in the pharma example) stated separately; exact coverage depends on the spectrometer and probe. [S01 p.15]

## Risks and uncertainty

- Material build-up on the ATR crystal would bias or block the signal, since ATR samples only a surface layer a few microns deep. [S01 p.13]
- No supplied source states a temperature rating for MIR fibers, ATR probes or their seals, so the hot-melt compatibility is unverified. [S02 p.9]
- Raman signal may be swamped by fluorescence from the polymer or additives, which must be checked on real melt samples. [S01 p.11]

## Feasibility plan

- **Next step:** Run the multi-method lab trial on representative melt samples with MIR-ATR and Raman probes against the offline assay, after confirming melt temperature and access geometry.
- **Reference measurements:** Offline assays supplied by the customer (API concentration per sample).
- **Decision basis:** Customer-defined maximum prediction error for API concentration and minimum detectable composition change, validated on held-out runs.
