# Short brief: Blend potency in a tablet press feed frame

*Short view built by code from the saved run JSON (no model call). Full answer: [D01.md](../D01.md).*

**Decision status:** Provisional feasibility assessment

Test NIR diffuse reflection first: it is the standard fiber-probe route for opaque powders and has published feed-frame precedent for blend potency. Compare it with a Raman fiber probe on the same blend; combine the two only if a single method misses your accuracy target. Open points: your API range and accuracy needs, excipient interferences, and the reference lab method for calibration.

## Requirements

**Stated by the customer:** "We manufacture tablets from a powder blend containing an active pharmaceutical ingredient and excipients."; "Laboratory checks tell us whether the blend is on target, but results arrive after the material has moved through the press."; "We want to assess whether a fiber based measurement in the feed frame could follow API concentration changes."; "Our first pilot should compare reasonable measurement options before we commit to production equipment."

**Other key unknowns:** Excipient composition and possible spectral interferences with the API; Powder flow behaviour and particle size in the feed frame.

## Clarification questions

1. What API concentration range must be tracked, and what accuracy and response time do you need in the feed frame?
2. Which excipients are in the blend, and do any have strong NIR or Raman bands overlapping the API?
3. What reference laboratory method (e.g. HPLC) and sampling rate can you provide for calibration samples taken during the pilot?

## Recommendation

## Measurement concept

NIR diffuse-reflection fiber probe with the tip in contact with or a few millimetres above the moving powder bed in the feed frame; Raman fiber probe at the same spot for the comparison test. **Location:** Feed frame of the rotary tablet press, before die filling. **Fiber link:** Vis-NIR silica fibers; for Raman, metal-coated low-OH silica fibers free of polymer-jacket fluorescence. Actual cable length depends on the selected configuration and is not stated in the sources. [S01 p.15; S02 p.7]

## Spectral range

- **NIR:** Nominal NIR range 780-2500 nm; the usable range depends on the spectrometer, fiber and probe actually selected. [S01 p.10]
- **Raman:** Raman shift in cm-1 with laser excitation stated separately; the published feed-frame example used 785 nm excitation. Actual usable shift range depends on the selected spectrometer and probe. [S01 p.15]

## Risks and uncertainty

- The published feed-frame results are for other blends; this customer's API and excipients may give weaker or overlapping signals.
- NIR reflection signals depend on surface characteristics and particle size, so physical variation in the feed frame can mimic composition change. [S01 p.17]
- Raman signals may be swamped by fluorescence from excipients or the API on the real blend. [S01 p.11]

## Feasibility plan

- **Next step:** Run the multi-method lab pilot on representative blend samples with independent reference assays, then a short feed-frame trial against customer-agreed go/no-go criteria.
- **Reference measurements:** Independent laboratory API assay on pilot samples, taken at a frequency agreed with the customer.
- **Decision basis:** Go/no-go on whether the in-line prediction meets the customer's stated accuracy and response-time targets on held-out runs; the numerical criteria come from the customer.
