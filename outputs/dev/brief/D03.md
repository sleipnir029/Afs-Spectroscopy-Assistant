# Short brief: A clear whey stream before concentration

*Short view built by code from the saved run JSON (no model call). Full answer: [D03.md](../D03.md).*

**Decision status:** Provisional feasibility assessment

Test NIR first: a transmission or transflection probe is the documented geometry for clear whey streams, while diffuse reflection provides a fallback if turbidity increases. We recommend testing both probe geometries on representative whey samples spanning clear and turbid conditions against laboratory protein reference data. If single NIR techniques cannot handle heavy turbidity, MIR ATR remains a conditional multi-method option. Open points: protein range, turbidity cause and frequency, and process temperature.

## Requirements

**Stated by the customer:** "Our dairy process includes a relatively clear whey stream before a concentration stage."; "laboratory results used as the reference"; "The stream can become more turbid under some operating conditions."; "track protein concentration as the stream changes"

**Other key unknowns:** Cause, frequency and duration of the turbid episodes; Laboratory reference method and its sampling frequency.

## Clarification questions

1. What is the protein concentration range, and how large and how fast are the changes to be tracked?
2. What causes the turbid episodes (e.g. fat, precipitated protein, air) and how often do they occur?
3. What are the process temperature, pressure and the available installation point and pipe dimensions?

## Measurement concept

Immersion or flow-cell NIR transflection probe at a representative point in the whey line before the concentration stage; diffuse reflection or MIR ATR as comparators. **Location:** In the whey stream upstream of the concentration stage, at a point representative of the flow. **Fiber link:** Vis-NIR silica fibers; the usable range depends on the spectrometer, fiber and probe actually selected. [S01 p.10; S02 p.6]

## Spectral range

- **NIR:** Nominal NIR range 780-2500 nm; usable range depends on the spectrometer, fiber and probe actually selected. [S01 p.10]

## Risks and uncertainty

- The turbid state may saturate a transmission/transflection signal, so the pathlength or geometry may need to change between states. [S01 p.18]
- Reflection signals respond to surface characteristics and particle or globule size, so physical variation could mimic protein changes unless the calibration covers it. [S01 p.17]
- The calibration is specific to this whey matrix; changes in composition, fat or temperature require re-validation. [S01 p.10]

## Feasibility plan

- **Next step:** Test transflection and reflection NIR probes on representative clear and turbid whey samples against laboratory protein reference measurements.
- **Reference measurements:** Laboratory protein analysis on every calibration and validation sample, taken at matched temperature.
- **Decision basis:** Customer-defined maximum prediction error for protein in each optical state (clear and turbid) and acceptable response time; these numbers must come from the customer.
