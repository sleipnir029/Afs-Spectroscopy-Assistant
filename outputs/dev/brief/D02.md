## Measurement concept

Flush-mounted NIR diffuse-reflection fiber probe in the process pipe, light delivered and backscattered light collected through the probe; a transflection probe tested in parallel at the same sampling point. **Location:** A well-mixed point on the processing line, downstream of composition changes the customer wants to detect early. **Fiber link:** Vis-NIR silica fibers (nominal transmission to about 2400 nm per S02 p.6); the usable range depends on the spectrometer, fiber and probe actually selected. [S01 p.17; S02 p.6]

## Spectral range

- **NIR:** Nominal NIR method range 780-2500 nm; the usable range depends on the spectrometer, fiber and probe actually selected. [S01 p.10]

## Risks and uncertainty

- Texture and fat-globule variation can mimic composition change in reflection spectra, so scatter correction (e.g. MSC/SNV) and a texture-representative calibration are essential. [S01 p.17]
- Temperature variation shifts the spectra and can bias the fat and protein predictions if not covered in the calibration.
- NIR calibrations are specific to the product matrix, so any recipe or process change requires re-validation of the model. [REF §5]

## Feasibility plan

- **Next step:** Run the bench comparison of NIR reflection and transflection probes on representative samples with laboratory reference values, after confirming temperature range, accuracy targets and mounting constraints.
- **Reference measurements:** Existing laboratory fat and protein analysis on every calibration and validation sample.
- **Decision basis:** Prediction error on held-out runs must meet the customer's stated accuracy targets for fat and protein; those targets are to be confirmed.
