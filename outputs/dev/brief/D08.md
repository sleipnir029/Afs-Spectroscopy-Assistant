## Measurement concept

MIR ATR fiber probe with a ZrO2 or diamond ATR tip in contact with the tissue; fluorescence via a fiber probe at the same or (preferably) the same combined probe head. **Location:** Laboratory bench, probe tip pressed onto defined marked positions on each tissue sample. **Fiber link:** MIR channel needs CIR or PIR fiber (silica does not transmit mid-IR); fluorescence channel uses silica fibers; short lab lengths are sufficient. [S02 p.6; S04 p.2]

## Spectral range

- **MIR:** Nominal mid-IR range 4000-400 cm-1; the usable range depends on the selected ATR crystal, CIR/PIR fiber and spectrometer. [S01 p.9]
- **Fluorescence:** Emission range depends on the specific fluorophores present and the excitation wavelength chosen in the UV and visible regions; the usable range depends on the instrument, fiber, and probe optics. [S01 p.12]

## Risks and uncertainty

- ATR reads only a few micrometres of surface, so on heterogeneous tissue the classification may reflect local surface composition rather than tissue type. [S01 p.13]
- If the fluorescence channel excites the wrong fluorophores or the tissues lack fluorescent components, its contribution may be negligible. [S01 p.5]
- With few samples per tissue type, fused or single-method classification models can overfit and validation statistics become unreliable. [S03 p.6]

## Feasibility plan

- **Next step:** Run the laboratory comparison on representative chicken tissue samples using sequential single-method measurements, with the combined probe tested if available.
- **Reference measurements:** Tissue type per sample and position, confirmed independently of the spectra (visual/histological labelling).
- **Decision basis:** Per-method and fused classification performance on held-out samples, judged against the accuracy criteria the group defines in question 1.
