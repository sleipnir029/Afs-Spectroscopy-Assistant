## Measurement concept

NIR transmission or transflection probe on the whey line; NIR diffuse-reflection probe on the concentrate line; both candidates compared, with a single transflection probe as a possible simplification. **Location:** Flush-mounted in-line insertion in each stream, at a point representative of the flow. **Fiber link:** Vis-NIR silica fibers connect the probes to the spectrometer; the usable range depends on the selected instrument, fiber and probe. [S02 p.6; S01 p.17]

## Spectral range

- **NIR:** Nominal NIR range 780-2500 nm; the usable range depends on the spectrometer, fiber and probe actually selected. [S01 p.10]

## Risks and uncertainty

- If the whey is less clear or more variable in fat content than assumed, transmission may saturate and reflection or transflection may be needed instead. [S01 p.18]
- Reflection signals on the concentrate may track fat globule size and surface effects rather than composition, so physical variation can mimic composition change. [S01 p.17]
- NIR bands are broad and overlapping, so quantitative protein and fat results depend entirely on a matrix-specific chemometric calibration that must be built and validated per stream. [S01 p.10]

## Feasibility plan

- **Next step:** Run the comparison experiment: measure representative samples of both streams with NIR transmission/transflection and reflection probes, calibrate against laboratory protein and fat results, and decide on the agreed accuracy criteria.
- **Reference measurements:** Laboratory protein and fat analyses on every pilot sample, taken at matched times with the spectral measurements.
- **Decision basis:** Per-stream prediction error for protein and fat on held-out runs, against accuracy limits agreed with the customer.
