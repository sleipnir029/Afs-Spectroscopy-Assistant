## Measurement concept

Laboratory trial first: immersion Raman probe and MIR-ATR fiber probe tested on the same representative liquid samples; NIR transmission/transflection added if the liquid proves clear. **Location:** Bench-top lab trial on archived or grab samples; in-line installation point decided only after the method is selected. **Fiber link:** Silica fibers for Raman (visible/NIR excitation); mid-IR requires CIR or PIR fibers, whose achievable link length is an open question - long (100-300 m) cables are documented only for low-OH silica fibers. [S02 p.6; S02 p.7]

## Spectral range

- **Raman:** Raman shift in cm-1 with laser excitation wavelength stated separately; nominal range depends on the selected spectrometer and probe. [S01 p.11]
- **MIR:** Nominal mid-IR range 4000-400 cm-1; the usable range depends on the selected fiber and ATR crystal. [S01 p.9]
- **NIR:** Nominal NIR range 780-2500 nm; the usable range depends on the instrument, fiber and probe actually selected. [S01 p.10]

## Risks and uncertainty

- The contaminant may have weak Raman activity or sit below the detection limit of any optical method, making spectroscopy the wrong tool. (related AFS source: S01 p.11, "signals, which are several orders of magnitude weaker than IR absorption peaks"; not checked against this sentence)
- Fluorescence background from the matrix or from polymer-jacketed fibers can swamp the Raman signal on real samples. (related AFS source: S02 p.7, "Free of Polymer jacket fluorescence for Raman and Fluo- Spectroscopy"; not checked against this sentence)
- If the liquid is opaque or strongly absorbing, NIR transmission and UV-Vis transmission will fail and only ATR or reflection geometries remain. (related AFS source: S01 p.18, "it fails when the milk becomes too "thick" or fat-rich, as the light cannot penetrate the sample."; not checked against this sentence)

## Feasibility plan
